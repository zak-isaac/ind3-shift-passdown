1. What This System Does

The IND3 Reliability Passdown System is the team's digital shift handover tool. Instead of writing passdowns by hand or copying from emails, technicians upload their Cetaris work orders directly and the system formats everything into a clean report that the next shift can read in under a minute.

Every entry that comes in gets recorded in real time. The MST sees a live feed of all activity, can review and clean it up, and generates the final report with a single click. The system also keeps a historical record of every shift, which feeds into an analytics dashboard showing which assets get worked on most often, how frequently incidents happen, and who the most active technicians are.

The goal is simple: nothing gets lost between shifts, and nobody has to write the same information twice.

3. Technician Workflow

When you arrive for your shift, open the passdown URL on your phone or any computer on the facility network. The home screen shows the currently active shift. Enter your associate number — the six-digit number on your badge — and tap Check In. The system recognizes your number and logs you into your dashboard.

Your dashboard shows two things: a list of everything you have already submitted this shift, and the Cetaris upload section. The list is read-only once submitted. If you need something changed, let your MST know and they can edit it from their dashboard.

At the top of the dashboard you will also see any carry-over items from the previous shift. These are assets the last shift flagged as needing follow-up. Read through them before you start your shift so you already know what needs attention.

If your associate number is not recognized, your number is not on the roster. Ask your MST to add you in the Admin settings.

4. Uploading from Cetaris

The upload section is how your work orders get into the passdown. You export a CSV from Cetaris at the end of your shift and drag it into the upload zone on your dashboard. The system reads every work order in the file and creates an entry for each one automatically.

Exporting from Cetaris

1
Log in to Cetaris and navigate to your work orders.
2
Filter by your name and the current date so you are only looking at your shift's work.
3
Use the Export option to download a CSV file to your device.
4
Go back to the passdown and drag that file into the upload zone, or tap it to browse.
5
The system shows a summary of how many work orders were imported and how many were PMs.

Preventive Maintenance work orders (labeled as "Prev Maintenance" in Cetaris) are automatically recognized and counted separately. They do not appear as individual happenings in the passdown report but are tallied and shown as a PM total.

If you need to re-upload the same file because you added more WOs to it, that is fine. The system knows which entries belong to you, so re-uploading updates your existing entries instead of creating duplicates. If two technicians both work on the same WO number, the system keeps both entries but only shows one line for that WO in the final report.

The Cetaris "Tech Remarks" field is used as the description for each entry. Write your remarks in Cetaris before you export so they carry through automatically.

5. Opening a Shift

Only the MST can open and close shifts. From the home screen, select MST Login and enter your MST code. Once inside the dashboard, click Open New Shift, choose the correct shift type, enter your name, and the shift goes live. Technicians can check in immediately once the shift is open.

6. The Live Feed

The MST dashboard has a live feed that shows every entry as it comes in. The page does not need to be refreshed. A WebSocket connection keeps it updated in real time, so the moment a tech uploads their Cetaris file, those entries appear on your screen.

Each card in the feed shows the technician's name and initial, the time the entry was submitted, the asset ID, and the issue description. A colored stripe runs down the left side of each card: red means the asset is Down, yellow means Degraded, and green means Monitor. The priority badge on the right side of the card says the same thing in text. The status bar at the top of the feed shows a running count of how many assets are in each state.

Entries that the MST has excluded from the report remain visible in the feed but are grayed out with strikethrough text and a red "Excluded from report" label. This way nothing disappears unexpectedly and the exclusion can always be reversed.

If the green live dot at the top of the feed disappears, the WebSocket connection dropped. Refresh the page to reconnect. This usually means the network briefly went offline.

7. MST Entry Controls

Every entry card in the live feed has four small icon buttons stacked on the right side. These are only visible and functional for the MST.

IconActionWhat it does
✏️ Edit Opens an inline edit form inside the card. The current issue text appears in a text box so you can correct it, add detail, or clean up the wording. Saving the change immediately updates the card in the feed and updates what will appear in the report. Cancel returns the card to its original view without saving.
🚫 Exclude Marks the entry so it does not appear in the generated report. The entry stays visible in the feed (grayed out) so you always know it exists. Use this for entries where a tech left notes but the item is not worth reporting. Clicking the button again re-includes the entry.
📌 Carry-Over Flags this asset for the next shift to follow up on. The button turns orange to confirm it is on. Carry-over items appear in a highlighted panel at the top of every technician's dashboard when the next shift opens, and they are listed in a dedicated section of the report.
🗑️ Delete Permanently removes the entry. The system will ask you to confirm before deleting. There is no undo. Only use this for entries that should not exist at all. If you just want to hide something from the report, use Exclude instead.

8. Generating the Report

When the shift is wrapping up, review the live feed one more time. Make sure excluded entries are marked, carry-over flags are set, and any entries with unclear descriptions have been edited. Once everything looks right, click Generate Report.

The report is built instantly from the current state of the shift and shown in a preview. From there you can copy it to the clipboard and paste it into Teams or email, or open the print view for a formatted document version. The report is also saved to the system's history so it can be retrieved later.

You can generate the report more than once if needed. Each time you generate, the system saves a new snapshot. The most recent generation is the official record. Generating the report does not close the shift, so you can continue making changes and re-generating if late entries come in.

9. How the Report Is Formatted

The report is structured so the next shift can scan through it quickly. At the top is the facility, date, shift type, and who prepared it. Then the sections follow in order.

The Shift Happenings section is the most important one. Each entry appears on its own line with the asset ID in bold, followed by the description and the work order number in parentheses if one exists. Here is what a few lines look like in practice:

PARKING0002: Yard buddy and also assisting with installing the horn and the side light. (WO #27779109).
VOIDFILL0101: Inspected the area and noticed the taper was not seated properly causing the blade to have no spring tension. Lifted the bottom taper, reinserted it correctly, cleared fault, set machine to autofill and it ran clean. Monitored. (WO #27798811).
CR0005: Unplugged the machine, waited a few minutes then plugged it back up and it is back working.

When two technicians work on the same work order number, the report shows that WO only once. The duplicates are merged so the next shift does not see the same asset listed multiple times for the same job. The PM total is shown as a single summary line rather than listing every individual PM work order.

After Shift Happenings, the report includes Reliable Operations for assets that were returned to service, Out of Service for anything still down, and the PM count. Any carry-over items from the previous shift appear in a highlighted section at the top.

10. Carry-Over

A carry-over is an asset that was worked on during the current shift but needs the next shift to check on it. The MST sets carry-over flags in the live feed before generating the report.

When the next shift opens and any technician logs in, their dashboard shows a carry-over panel at the top with the asset ID, the original issue description, and which shift it came from. This means the incoming technicians know exactly what to look at before they even start walking the floor. The same items are listed in the carry-over section of the generated report.

11. Analytics Dashboard

The Analytics page is accessible from the Analytics link in the MST dashboard navigation. It pulls data across all recorded shifts and presents it in a set of charts and tables.

The top row of stat cards shows total shifts logged, total work orders recorded, total PMs completed, and total Down events across all time. Below that, a bar chart shows the fifteen assets with the most work orders, with the top three highlighted in red as chronic problem assets. A doughnut chart breaks down the ratio of Down, Degraded, and Monitor entries across all shifts.

The trend charts show work order count and down events per shift for the last thirty completed shifts, and PM count per shift for the same period. These let you see whether workload is increasing, whether down events are clustering around certain dates, and how consistently the team is keeping up with preventive maintenance.

The repeat problem assets table lists every asset that has appeared in more than one distinct shift, ranked by how many shifts it has appeared in. An asset that shows up across five different shifts is a signal that there is an unresolved root cause worth investigating. The top technicians chart shows entry count per technician, which reflects who is documenting their work thoroughly.

At the bottom of the page, the executive insights strip calls out the single most problematic asset, the most active technician, and the percentage of work orders that were Down events.

12. Admin Settings

The Admin page is accessible from the gear icon in the MST dashboard. It has two sections.

Associate Roster

This section shows every associate who can log into the system. The roster is sourced from the Drax Associates spreadsheet in OneDrive. When someone is added or removed from that spreadsheet, click Re-sync from Drax and the system will pull the latest version, update the database, and refresh the table on screen immediately. The re-sync preserves any MST flags that were set manually so those are never accidentally cleared.

You can also add an associate directly in this page without going through the spreadsheet. Fill in their associate number, full name, and shift, check the MST box if they should have lead access, and click Add. The MST toggle on each row lets you grant or remove lead access for any associate without changing the Drax file.

A technician who is not on the roster cannot log in. If someone is having trouble checking in, check that their associate number appears in the roster table and that it matches exactly what is on their badge.

Teams Notifications

Paste an incoming webhook URL from your Teams channel here and save it. Once active, the system will post a card to that Teams channel whenever a technician submits an entry with a Down or Degraded priority. This means the MST gets a notification in Teams the moment something goes down, without needing to keep the dashboard open on screen.

13. Glossary

TermMeaning
PassdownThe shift handover document that summarizes everything that happened and passes critical information to the next shift.
MSTThe shift lead. MSTs have full access to the system including editing entries, generating reports, and the admin panel.
CetarisThe CMMS (Computerized Maintenance Management System) used at IND3. Technicians export work orders from Cetaris and upload the CSV to the passdown system.
WOWork Order. A tracked job in Cetaris tied to a specific asset and technician. WOs have unique numbers used to identify them in the passdown.
PMPreventive Maintenance. Scheduled routine work. PMs are counted in the report total but not listed individually as happenings.
Carry-OverAn asset flagged by the MST as needing follow-up by the next shift.
ExcludedAn entry that has been marked by the MST to not appear in the generated report. It still exists in the system and can be re-included at any time.
Live FeedThe real-time activity stream on the MST dashboard. Updates automatically as technicians upload entries.
DownThe asset is completely out of service and not running.
DegradedThe asset is running but at reduced capacity or with a known problem.
MonitorThe issue was addressed and the asset is running, but it should be watched for signs of recurrence.
RTSReturn to Service. The asset was down and is now fully operational again.
OOSOut of Service. The asset is still down at shift end and has not been returned to service.
DraxThe shared Associates spreadsheet in OneDrive that the system uses as the source of truth for the associate roster.
Associate NumberThe numeric ID on a technician's badge. Used to log into the system. No password is required.
Repeat OffenderAn asset that appears across multiple distinct shifts in the analytics. A candidate for root-cause investigation.
